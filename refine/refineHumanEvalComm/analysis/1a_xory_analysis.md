# 1a manipulation: contradictory "X or Y" descriptions

The 1a category of HumanEvalComm injects ambiguity into a task description. A recurring pattern is a contradictory disjunction of the form "do X or Y", where the two options are mutually exclusive and only one matches the reference solution (for example "longest or shortest", "largest or smallest"). These are the most artificial of the 1a descriptions: a programmer specifying a function commits to a single behaviour, so stating two opposing goals as interchangeable alternatives is not how genuine underspecification arises in practice. It reads as contradiction rather than ambiguity, and it inflates measured incoherence in a way that does not reflect naturally ambiguous prompts. For this reason the "X or Y" descriptions are excluded from the task subset. The classification of the task descriptions was done by Claude Opus 4.8 and a subset of tasks was manually verified.

Example (task 10, make_palindrome):

> Find the longest or shortest palindrome that begins with a supplied string.

Of the 164 descriptions, 105 are classified as "X or Y" and 59 are not.
